Single-cell meta-analysis of galectin expression in oral squamous cell carcinoma

We reanalyzed three public oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) single-cell RNA-seq cohorts with Seurat, focusing on the galectin family and on LGALS7 (galectin-7). The cohorts were GSE181919 (Choi et al.; 10x, oral-cavity subset: 10 primary tumors, 3 normal mucosae, and 4 leukoplakias; 24,156 cells), GSE103322 (Puram et al.; Smart-seq2; 5,902 cells from primary tumors and lymph-node metastases), and GSE172577 (Peng et al.; 10x; 57,907 cells after quality control from six primary tumors). Comparisons of detection rate and mean expression were made at the sample level (one value per sample; Wilcoxon tests), so that cells from the same tumor were not treated as independent observations. Mean expression was not compared across platforms, because GSE103322 is stored as log2(TPM/10+1) whereas the 10x cohorts are log1p-normalized counts.

Galectins occupy distinct compartments of the OSCC microenvironment. In primary tumors, LGALS1 and LGALS3 were broadly detected across malignant, stromal, and immune cells (for example, fibroblast LGALS1 detection was 100% in GSE181919, 91% in GSE103322, and 97% in GSE172577). LGALS2 and LGALS9 were enriched in myeloid and endothelial cells rather than in the epithelium: in GSE181919 primary tumors, LGALS2 was detected in 61% of macrophages versus 5% of malignant cells, and LGALS9 in 70% of macrophages and 63% of endothelial cells versus 18% of malignant cells. LGALS4 was rarely detected. LGALS7 and its paralog LGALS7B showed the opposite pattern and were concentrated in malignant or epithelial cells.

Galectin-7 itself was an epithelial marker in every cohort. In GSE181919 oral-cavity primary tumors, LGALS7 was detected in 53.3% of malignant cells (1,376 cells; mean log-normalized expression 0.59) compared with 1.7% of T cells, 1.0% of fibroblasts, 0.6% of macrophages, and 0.3% of endothelial cells. Paired within-tumor tests confirmed higher detection and higher mean expression in malignant cells than in T cells (p = 0.0059), B/plasma cells (p = 0.014), fibroblasts (p = 0.014), macrophages (p = 0.009), and dendritic cells (p = 0.036). LGALS7B was detected in 71.9% of the same malignant cells. Normal oral epithelium was lower (5.5% of 199 epithelial cells across three samples; sample-level median detection 0%; percent detected p = 0.022 versus OSCC, mean expression p = 0.11). That normal comparison is underpowered and should not be read as evidence that normal mucosa lacks galectin-7. Leukoplakia epithelium was as high as carcinoma (63.9% of 183 cells; percent detected p = 0.94 versus OSCC). In GSE103322, LGALS7 was detected in 78.2% of primary malignant cells (mean 3.98 on the author log2(TPM/10+1) scale; n = 1,427) and 62.6% of lymph-node malignant cells (n = 788), with no sample-level difference between primary and nodal tumor cells (p = 0.94–1.0). Detection in primary-tumor fibroblasts and T cells was 0.5% and 0.3% (paired p = 0.0039 and 0.036). In GSE172577, where epithelial cells were defined by marker scores rather than by copy-number-confirmed malignancy, LGALS7 was detected in 36.9% of epithelial cells (n = 41,187; mean 0.55) versus 8.7% of endothelial cells and about 2–3% of T cells, myeloid cells, and fibroblasts (paired epithelial versus fibroblast, T cell, or myeloid p = 0.036).

Within tumor epithelial cells, Spearman correlation linked LGALS7 to keratinocyte state rather than to proliferation or EMT. In GSE181919 malignant cells (n = 1,376), LGALS7 correlated with a basal keratin program (KRT15, KRT5, KRT14, TP63, COL17A1, ITGA6; rho = 0.35) and with epithelial adhesion genes (rho = 0.38; PKP1 rho = 0.45, JUP rho = 0.41, DSG3 rho = 0.27). The suprabasal differentiation program was only weakly related (rho = 0.07). Proliferation was inversely related (rho = −0.13), and this inverse relationship was stronger among LGALS7-detected cells (rho = −0.30). The EMT module was near zero (rho = 0.01), and an invasion module was weakly negative (rho = −0.08). Among signaling genes, CCND1 was positively correlated (rho = 0.33) and CDKN2A and MYC were negatively correlated (rho = −0.24 and −0.12). In GSE103322 malignant cells (n = 2,215), the basal association was stronger (rho = 0.50; KRT14 rho = 0.63, COL17A1 rho = 0.50, KRT5 rho = 0.48) and the differentiation program was also positive (rho = 0.30; KRT13 rho = 0.31). LGALS7 additionally tracked an invasive keratin program (rho = 0.40), driven by LAMC2 (rho = 0.41), PTHLH (rho = 0.21), and MMP10 (rho = 0.17), and correlated with EGFR (rho = 0.25). The proliferation module was essentially uncorrelated (rho = −0.02). The adhesion module was near zero because EPCAM was negatively correlated (rho = −0.44) while DSG3 was positively correlated (rho = 0.40). In GSE172577 marker-defined tumor epithelium (n = 41,187), LGALS7 correlated with suprabasal differentiation (rho = 0.35; KRT13 rho = 0.44, SPINK5 rho = 0.38, KRT4 rho = 0.31, IVL rho = 0.29), was essentially uncorrelated with the basal module as a whole (rho = 0.03, although KRT5 itself was rho = 0.32), and was negatively correlated with the invasion module (rho = −0.28) and weakly with EMT (rho = −0.08). A modest positive correlation with an oncogenic-signaling module (rho = 0.16) was led by MYC (rho = 0.26) and FOS (rho = 0.23). Within LGALS7-detected Peng cells the differentiation correlation fell to rho = 0.03, indicating that part of the all-cell association reflects joint detection of LGALS7 and differentiation genes. LGALS7B remained the closest galectin paralog (rho = 0.51 in all Peng epithelial cells and 0.67 in LGALS7-detected cells; rho = 0.22 and 0.32 in GSE181919 and GSE103322 malignant cells).

Slingshot pseudotime was fit inside tumor epithelial or malignant cells only, with the curve started at the cluster with the highest basal-minus-differentiated score and ended at the cluster with the lowest score. This orders expression states within tumor epithelium; it is not a normal-to-cancer timeline and it is not clock time. The three cohorts did not return the same biological axis. In GSE172577 (3,580 cells retained on the curve after downsampling), pseudotime ran from basal toward differentiated keratinocytes (basal score rho = −0.46; differentiated score rho = 0.58), and LGALS7 increased along that path (rho = 0.42) with a modest correlation to the differentiated score (rho = 0.18) and no correlation to the basal score (rho = 0.02). In GSE181919 (663 cells on the curve), the inferred direction ran toward the basal program (basal rho = 0.47; differentiated rho = −0.41), and LGALS7 rose with pseudotime (rho = 0.59) and with the basal score (rho = 0.47). In GSE103322 (1,443 cells), both the basal score (rho = −0.71) and the differentiated score (rho = −0.34) declined along pseudotime, so the curve was not a clean basal-to-suprabasal path; LGALS7 declined along the curve (rho = −0.59) while remaining positively correlated with the basal score (rho = 0.51) and, more moderately, with the differentiated score (rho = 0.36).

Taken together, galectin-7 is a malignant and epithelial feature of OSCC, separable from the stromal and immune galectins LGALS1, LGALS2, LGALS3, and LGALS9. Inside tumor epithelium it co-varies with keratinocyte identity. That identity is basal and adhesion-associated in GSE181919, basal plus an invasive keratin program in GSE103322, and suprabasal in GSE172577. LGALS7 is not a marker of proliferation or of canonical EMT. A basal-to-differentiated pseudotime link is supported in the Peng cohort, where LGALS7 increases as epithelial cells move away from the basal state, and is not recovered as a single shared trajectory in the Choi or Puram malignant cells.
